CANDIDATE NEXT RFT-VS-SFT USE CASE: SUPPLY CHAIN / RETAIL DISRUPTION RECOVERY PLANNING
(Compiled 2026-07-22, from Phase 4 retrospective discussion in retail-hillclimb-showcase)
=======================================================================================

This document captures, in full, the proposed next fine-tuning showcase use
case discussed after concluding Phase 4 (fulfillment-planning RFT vs SFT,
where SFT won 0.864 vs RFT's 0.428 -- see phases/phase4/logs/2026-07-21_1300/
capture_run_analysis.md for that project's full analysis). It is provided as
a standalone reference so this proposal can be picked up independently of
that log.

Nothing here has been built or run yet. This is a design proposal plus a
mandatory pre-flight pilot checklist that must pass BEFORE committing to any
paid fine-tuning job.


===========================================================================
PART 1 — RECOMMENDED USE CASE (FULL VERSION): MULTI-WAREHOUSE DISRUPTION
RECOVERY PLANNING
===========================================================================

RECOMMENDED USE CASE

Peak-season order recovery after a supply-chain disruption

A retailer has:
- 5 distribution centers
- hundreds of units of inventory spread unevenly across them
- 30-50 customer orders with different SLAs and priorities
- carrier capacity limits
- shipping and transfer costs
- substitute-product rules
- uncertain supplier replenishment dates
- the option to transfer, expedite, substitute, split, defer, or cancel orders

Then a disruption occurs:
- one distribution center loses capacity
- a supplier shipment is delayed
- a carrier lane becomes constrained
- demand exceeds the remaining stock

The model must produce a COMPLETE RECOVERY PLAN deciding:
- which warehouse fulfills each order
- whether to split an order
- whether to substitute an item
- whether to transfer inventory between warehouses
- which shipments to expedite
- which low-priority orders to defer
- how to respect inventory, labor, carrier, budget, and SLA constraints

This is not merely "choose one of three labels." It is a combinatorial
planning problem with many potentially good answers.

WHY RFT FITS

1. Correct plans are easy to verify
Given a proposed plan, a deterministic simulator can quickly answer:
- Did it allocate inventory that does not exist?
- Did it exceed warehouse or carrier capacity?
- Did it violate substitution rules?
- Did it miss delivery SLAs?
- What was the shipping and transfer cost?
- How many priority orders were fulfilled?
- How much revenue or margin was preserved?
- How much inventory imbalance remained?
- How did the plan perform under the disruption simulation?
Verification is straightforward arithmetic and simulation.

2. Good plans are difficult to generate
Finding a high-quality plan requires searching across a huge number of
combinations: warehouse assignment, carrier selection, substitutions,
splits, transfers, expedite decisions, defer/cancel decisions. A plan is
cheap to score but hard to discover. That is the RFT condition that was
missing in the original retail fulfillment experiment.

3. There is no single golden answer
Two plans can both be excellent:
- Plan A may expedite premium orders and defer low-margin orders.
- Plan B may transfer inventory first and avoid expensive expedites.
- Plan C may substitute products and preserve more revenue.
- Plan D may accept slightly lower revenue to reduce risk and cost.
SFT must be given one particular plan to imitate. RFT can reward any plan
that satisfies the constraints and achieves a strong outcome.

4. Reward can be continuous
The model need not produce a perfect plan before receiving useful feedback.
A candidate can improve gradually: fewer constraint violations, more orders
fulfilled, fewer late deliveries, lower cost, higher retained margin, better
robustness under disruption. That gives RFT a real hill to climb.

REWARD DESIGN

The simulator should first calculate whether the plan is feasible. Then it
should score business outcomes.

One possible score:

R = 0.35*S_service + 0.25*S_margin + 0.15*S_cost + 0.15*S_robustness + 0.10*S_balance

Where:
- S_service: weighted percentage of orders delivered within SLA
- S_margin: percentage of recoverable margin preserved
- S_cost: normalized shipping, transfer, and expedite efficiency
- S_robustness: performance across several fixed disruption simulations
- S_balance: avoidance of severe inventory depletion at one location

Hard constraint violations should impose caps:

| Violation                              | Maximum total reward |
|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------|
| Invalid JSON/schema                     | 0.00                  |
| Allocates nonexistent inventory         | 0.05                  |
| Exceeds warehouse/carrier capacity      | 0.10                  |
| Uses prohibited substitution            | 0.10                  |
| Exceeds budget                          | 0.20                  |
| Feasible but poor plan                  | Uncapped continuous   |

The caps prevent a plan from earning a competitive reward by exploiting one
attractive metric while being operationally impossible.

MAKING DISCOVERY EASY ENOUGH (CURRICULUM)

RFT needs enough early successes to learn. Do not immediately give it the
hardest possible network. Use a curriculum:

Level 1: Allocation only
- 2 warehouses; 8-10 orders; no transfers; deterministic delivery times;
  one disruption; allocate, substitute, or defer.
A reasonable base model should frequently generate feasible plans.

Level 2: Capacity and expedites
- 3 warehouses; 15-20 orders; carrier limits; expedite decisions; budget
  constraint.

Level 3: Transfers and multiple disruptions
- 4-5 warehouses; transfers; supplier delays; simultaneous warehouse and
  carrier disruption.

Level 4: Robust planning
Score each plan across several hidden but fixed disruption realizations.
The model must discover plans that remain good under uncertainty rather
than overfit to one forecast.

This could be implemented as successive o4-mini RFT jobs, continuing from
an earlier RFT checkpoint if Foundry permits that exact continuous-RFT
path (o4-mini -> o4-mini, same method, which IS allowed -- unlike crossing
from an SFT model's method to RFT, which is blocked; see Phase 4 log for
why gpt-4.1-mini -> o4-mini warm-start is not possible on this platform).

MAKING VERIFICATION RELIABLE

The simulator must be:
- deterministic for training, using fixed seeds
- independent of model-generated explanations
- based entirely on the structured plan
- fast enough to execute repeatedly
- validated against hand-calculated examples
- adversarially tested before training

Required adversarial tests include:
- duplicate allocations
- negative quantities
- allocating the same inventory twice
- invalid warehouse/SKU combinations
- budget manipulation
- omitting difficult orders
- splitting beyond requested quantity
- impossible transfers
- using unavailable substitutes
- exploiting rounding
- leaving required fields blank
- adding actions the simulator ignores

Every field in the output must either affect scoring or be rejected.
Ignored fields become reward-hacking surfaces.

WHY SFT HAS A REAL DISADVANTAGE HERE

To train SFT properly, we need high-quality recovery plans for every
training scenario. Where would those plans come from?
- Human supply-chain planners: expensive and slow.
- Teacher model: may produce plausible but suboptimal plans.
- MILP/constraint solver: possible, but if it cheaply produces optimal
  plans, then SFT again has good labels and RFT loses its structural
  advantage.
- Search/optimization over the simulator: potentially expensive, which
  demonstrates why verifier-guided RFT is useful.

Important rejection test:
IF an optimizer (e.g. OR-Tools, MILP) can cheaply generate near-optimal
plans for all training instances, DO NOT use this scenario to showcase
RFT over SFT.

Instead, make the environment include elements that are easy to simulate
but difficult to optimize directly: stochastic lead times, nonlinear
expedite pricing, service-level penalties, substitution-dependent demand
retention, cascading capacity effects, robust performance across multiple
fixed disruption realizations. Do not add arbitrary complexity merely to
handicap SFT -- these should represent real planning uncertainty.

REQUIRED PILOT BEFORE TRAINING (FULL VERSION)

Before spending on fine-tuning:
1. Build approximately 50 scenarios spanning Levels 1-3.
2. Deploy and evaluate the exact raw RFT base model.
3. Confirm at least approximately 20% of plans are fully feasible.
4. Confirm many additional plans receive meaningful partial scores.
5. Generate SFT labels with the proposed teacher.
6. Measure how often the teacher produces high-scoring plans on its first
   attempt.
7. Attempt solver-based labels and measure solution time and quality.
8. Test constant strategies such as "defer everything," "expedite
   everything," or "fulfill only premium orders."
9. Reject the reward if any constant strategy is competitive.
10. Freeze the final evaluation scenarios before training.

The candidate passes only if:
- the raw RFT model can discover feasible plans
- high-quality teacher demonstrations are materially harder to obtain than
  scores
- multiple distinct plans achieve strong scores
- no fixed shortcut performs well
- the simulator provides a smooth quality gradient

FAIR SFT COMPARISON

The SFT arm should receive the same training scenarios, but its targets
must be plans generated under a declared labeling budget, e.g.:
- one teacher attempt per scenario, OR
- equal dollar expenditure on SFT label generation and RFT training
- retain execution-filtered teacher plans
- report how many scenarios failed to obtain a high-quality label

Otherwise, unlimited search could produce perfect SFT labels and turn the
comparison into "expensive offline optimizer plus SFT versus RFT," which
answers a different question.

The evaluation must compare:
- raw RFT base
- RFT-trained model
- raw SFT base
- SFT-trained model
- optionally the teacher/solver used to label SFT

WHAT WOULD COUNT AS AN RFT WIN

RFT should not win merely by 0.01 on the mean score. A defensible win
would require:
- at least 5 percentage points over SFT on normalized business outcome
- paired confidence interval excluding zero
- higher performance on unseen disruption combinations
- no feasibility regression
- multiple solution patterns rather than one collapsed policy
- improvement over raw o4-mini
- explicit accounting of training and label-generation costs

The ideal finding would be:
"RFT learned several feasible recovery strategies and achieved higher
service and retained margin on unseen disruptions, while SFT remained
limited by the quality and diversity of the teacher-generated plans."

SECONDARY CANDIDATE: CONSTRAINED COMBINATORIAL OPTIMIZATION

A second strong candidate is generating feasible, high-quality schedules
or allocations where checking a proposal is cheap but finding a very good
proposal is hard. Examples:
- assign jobs to machines under capacity, deadline, and compatibility
  rules
- allocate orders across warehouses under stock, shipping cost, delivery
  SLA, and split-shipment constraints
- construct workforce shifts under coverage, skills, rest, and fairness
  rules

The grader should first enforce feasibility, then return a normalized
continuous objective score based on cost, lateness, number of splits, or
another explicit business objective. Many different plans must be capable
of scoring well.

This candidate is acceptable only if the score is much cheaper to compute
than the best plan. If an existing solver can cheaply generate optimal
plans for all training cases, SFT can receive perfect labels and the RFT
advantage disappears. Do not hide an easy solver merely to manufacture
scarcity; choose instance sizes where verification remains trivial but
optimization and demonstration generation are genuinely nontrivial.

BOUNDED PLATFORM-VALIDATION CANDIDATE: COUNTDOWN ARITHMETIC

The official-style Countdown task (combine supplied numbers and operations
to reach a target) is a useful low-risk validation of the RFT pipeline. It
has many valid expressions and a deterministic verifier. Suitable for
confirming that the platform, grader, metrics, and RFT job behave
correctly. Should be described as a toy reasoning demonstration, not
automatically as the final enterprise showcase -- it must still pass the
same raw-base, teacher-cost, common-base-model, and statistical gates
before claiming an RFT-over-SFT win. Easy Countdown distributions may
allow a teacher to generate cheap SFT demonstrations, weakening the
comparative case.

USE CASES TO REJECT FOR THE NEXT EXERCISE

Do not select another task dominated by:
- routing to one correct tool or label
- exact JSON/schema reproduction
- deterministic business-rule classification
- summarization or rewriting with cheap teacher outputs
- a grader model's subjective preference with no objective verifier
- a single safe response that receives broad partial credit
- near-zero raw-base success
- a static catalog or finite template set shared across train and
  evaluation
- an unavailable raw RFT base deployment
- different base models when the intended claim requires a controlled
  method-only comparison


===========================================================================
PART 2 — TWO-ARM EXPERIMENT DESIGN: gpt-4.1-mini SFT (labeled by gpt-5.5)
vs o4-mini RFT
===========================================================================

Proposed comparison:
- Option A: gpt-4.1-mini + SFT using plans generated by gpt-5.5, accepted
  and scored by the supply-chain simulator.
- Option B: o4-mini + RFT using the same scenario inputs and simulator as
  its reward function.

This compares the TWO DEPLOYABLE SOLUTIONS AVAILABLE IN FOUNDRY, not
training methods in isolation, because the base models differ.

WHAT IT ANSWERS

Can answer: "For supply-chain disruption recovery, which available
model/training package produces better plans: gpt-4.1-mini trained to
imitate validated gpt-5.5 plans, or o4-mini trained by exploring and
receiving simulator feedback?" That is an operationally meaningful
question.

Cannot cleanly answer: "Is RFT inherently better than SFT when everything
else is equal?" Foundry does not let us hold the base model constant. The
final result combines: base-model capability, training method, teacher
quality for SFT, reward quality and exploration for RFT.

EXACT EXPERIMENTAL DESIGN

Shared scenarios: generate one common collection of disruption-recovery
scenarios containing orders/priorities, inventory by warehouse, warehouse
handling capacity, carrier lanes and capacity, shipping/expedite costs,
allowed substitutions, transfers, deadlines, disruption events, total
budget.

Split by scenario family, not random rows:

| Split       | Suggested size | Purpose                          |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------------------------|
| Train       | 400-600         | Fine-tuning                       |
| Validation  | 80-120          | Training monitoring               |
| Evaluation  | 200+            | Final comparison                  |
| Pilot       | 50              | Go/no-go checks before paid training |

The evaluation set must contain unseen network layouts and disruption
combinations.

Identical output contract (both models return the same structured plan
schema: allocations / transfers / deferred_orders). Both must receive the
same system instructions, scenario information, and output schema during
evaluation.

Identical simulator: use exactly one deterministic simulator for
validating gpt-5.5 teacher labels, computing RFT rewards, and evaluating
both final models. That prevents the SFT labels and RFT rewards from
teaching different definitions of success.

SFT ARM

For every training scenario:
1. Ask gpt-5.5 to produce a complete recovery plan.
2. Run the plan through the simulator.
3. Record its feasibility and business-outcome score.
4. Retain only plans meeting a predetermined quality threshold (e.g. no
   hard constraint violations; normalized reward at least 0.80).
5. Fine-tune gpt-4.1-mini on those validated plans.

Do not silently give the teacher unlimited retries. Pick a labeling policy
before starting, e.g.: maximum three attempts per scenario; provide
simulator feedback after failed attempts; retain the highest-scoring
feasible attempt; report the attempts, cost, rejection rate, and final
label score distribution. This is important because the SFT arm includes
gpt-5.5 as a plan generator -- its quality and cost are part of that
solution.

RFT ARM

Give o4-mini only: scenario inputs, output schema, simulator-based reward.
It receives no teacher plans. The reward should:
1. Return zero or impose a hard cap for malformed or impossible plans.
2. Give partial credit for feasible progress.
3. Continuously reward higher service, margin, cost efficiency, and
   robustness.
4. Ensure constant shortcuts such as "defer everything" cannot score
   competitively.

The attraction of RFT is that it can discover plans better than the
teacher's demonstrations because it is not constrained to imitate them.

BASELINES ARE MANDATORY

Before fine-tuning, evaluate:
1. Raw gpt-4.1-mini.
2. Raw o4-mini.
3. gpt-5.5 teacher plans.

After fine-tuning, evaluate:
4. SFT gpt-4.1-mini.
5. RFT o4-mini.

| Arm                | Why it matters                          |
|---------------------|------------------------------------------|
| Raw gpt-4.1-mini     | Measures SFT improvement                 |
| Raw o4-mini          | Measures RFT improvement                 |
| gpt-5.5 teacher      | Measures the quality ceiling supplied to SFT |
| SFT gpt-4.1-mini     | Measures imitation quality                |
| RFT o4-mini          | Measures verifier-driven discovery        |

DO NOT PROCEED if raw o4-mini still cannot be deployed. Without its
baseline, we could compare final products, but we could not show that RFT
improved its model. That gap caused major ambiguity in the Phase 4
project.

TWO FAIRNESS VIEWS

1. Fixed-data comparison: give both arms the same training scenarios (SFT
   gets one validated teacher plan per usable scenario; RFT gets the same
   scenario without a target answer). Asks which learning setup extracts
   more quality from the same problem distribution.

2. Cost-aware comparison: track total cost (SFT teacher inference and
   retries + SFT training vs RFT training and grading), plus deployment
   and evaluation. Report quality versus total development cost. RFT may
   score higher but cost far more -- that matters operationally. Don't
   artificially force equal training dollars if it distorts the real
   workflow; report both quality and cost transparently.

WHEN THIS BECOMES RFT-FAVORABLE

Proceed only if the pilot confirms ALL of:
- Raw o4-mini produces at least ~20% fully feasible plans.
- Many imperfect o4-mini plans earn meaningful partial reward.
- gpt-5.5 cannot cheaply generate near-optimal plans on almost every first
  attempt.
- Multiple materially different recovery plans score highly.
- No constant strategy earns a competitive average score.
- The simulator is deterministic and survives adversarial tests.
- The train/evaluation split prevents scenario-template memorization.

The most important check is teacher difficulty. If gpt-5.5 produces a
0.95-quality plan on 95% of scenarios in one attempt, SFT again has cheap,
excellent demonstrations and RFT has little structural advantage --
reject or increase the planning difficulty before training.

Conversely, the candidate becomes compelling when gpt-5.5 frequently
produces feasible but merely adequate plans; obtaining excellent labels
requires repeated search or expensive optimization; raw o4-mini already
finds some feasible plans; the simulator can cheaply distinguish adequate
plans from excellent ones.

WHAT A REAL RFT WIN LOOKS LIKE

| Arm                | Illustrative result |
|---------------------|---------------------|
| gpt-5.5 teacher      | 0.78                |
| SFT gpt-4.1-mini     | 0.75                |
| Raw o4-mini          | 0.60                |
| RFT o4-mini          | 0.84 (winner)       |

With additional evidence that RFT:
- improves substantially over raw o4-mini
- exceeds SFT by at least five absolute points
- has a paired confidence interval excluding zero
- wins on unseen disruption combinations
- maintains feasibility
- uses several legitimate planning strategies
- discovers plans scoring above the gpt-5.5 teacher labels

That last result is especially important. If RFT beats BOTH SFT and its
teacher, it demonstrates the right advantage: "It discovered
higher-quality plans through outcome verification than were available in
the labeled demonstrations."

PART 3 — RING-FENCED MINIMUM VIABLE SLICE (BUILD THIS FIRST)
===========================================================================

MINIMUM VIABLE USE CASE

Disrupted inventory allocation for one fulfillment window

A distribution center becomes unavailable for the next 24 hours. The model
must reallocate a small batch of customer orders across the remaining
warehouses while deciding whether to:
- ship the requested SKU
- use an approved substitute
- expedite shipment
- or defer the order

This captures the central optimization problem without building a full
supply-chain simulator.

RING-FENCED SCOPE

Use:
- 3 warehouses
- 12-20 customer orders
- 4-6 SKUs
- one warehouse-capacity disruption
- inventory limits per warehouse
- shipping cost and delivery time per lane
- carrier capacity per warehouse
- customer priority and delivery deadline
- one approved substitute per selected SKU
- one total expedite budget

Do NOT initially include:
- warehouse-to-warehouse transfers
- supplier replenishment
- multiple planning days
- labor scheduling
- dynamic inventory updates
- stochastic demand
- multiple simultaneous disruptions
- external APIs, databases, or MCP servers
- natural-language explanations

The model returns only a structured allocation plan.

EXAMPLE SCENARIO

{
  "orders": [
    {
      "order_id": "O1",
      "sku": "A",
      "quantity": 2,
      "priority": 3,
      "deadline_hours": 24,
      "substitute_sku": "A2",
      "margin": 80
    }
  ],
  "warehouses": [
    {
      "warehouse_id": "W1",
      "inventory": {"A": 1, "A2": 5},
      "shipment_capacity": 7
    },
    {
      "warehouse_id": "W2",
      "inventory": {"A": 4, "A2": 0},
      "shipment_capacity": 5
    }
  ],
  "shipping": [
    {
      "warehouse_id": "W1",
      "standard_hours": 36,
      "standard_cost": 8,
      "expedite_hours": 18,
      "expedite_cost": 20
    }
  ],
  "expedite_budget": 100
}

The output is:

{
  "decisions": [
    {
      "order_id": "O1",
      "action": "ship",
      "warehouse_id": "W1",
      "sku": "A2",
      "quantity": 2,
      "shipping_mode": "expedite"
    }
  ]
}

Every order must have exactly one decision: "ship" or "defer".

WHY THIS IS REPRESENTATIVE

This small version preserves the important characteristics of the larger
use case:
- limited inventory must be shared among competing orders
- customer priorities conflict with cost
- substitutions increase the available choices
- expedites trade budget for service
- warehouse and carrier capacities couple the decisions
- there are many feasible plans rather than one mandatory answer
- improving one order can make another order worse

It excludes operational details that do not affect the RFT-versus-SFT
hypothesis.

SIMPLE VERIFIER

A plain Python function can evaluate a plan in milliseconds. It checks:
1. Every order appears exactly once.
2. The warehouse and SKU exist.
3. A substitution is allowed.
4. Total allocated inventory is available.
5. Warehouse shipment capacity is not exceeded.
6. Total expedite spending is within budget.
7. Shipped quantity matches the requested quantity.
8. Delivery time meets or misses the order deadline.

No external system or optimization library is required for grading.

REWARD

First calculate feasibility. Invalid plans should not receive attractive
partial rewards.

For feasible plans:

R = 0.55*S_service + 0.25*S_margin + 0.20*S_cost

Where:
- S_service is the priority-weighted fraction of orders delivered on time
- S_margin is the fraction of recoverable margin retained
- S_cost rewards lower shipping and expedite cost

Use caps for infeasible plans:

| Problem                              | Maximum score |
|----------------------------------------|---------------|
| Invalid JSON or missing orders          | 0.00          |
| Duplicate inventory allocation          | 0.05          |
| Invalid substitution                    | 0.10          |
| Capacity exceeded                       | 0.10          |
| Expedite budget exceeded                | 0.15          |
| Valid plan                              | 0.00-1.00     |

Also test these shortcut policies:
- defer every order
- expedite every order
- fulfill only priority customers
- always select the nearest warehouse
- always use substitutes
- process orders in input order

None should score close to a competent adaptive plan.

WHY DISCOVERY IS POSSIBLE

Start with instances where feasible plans are relatively easy to find:
- enough total inventory to serve approximately 70-85% of demand
- at least two warehouses can serve most orders
- only 20-30% of orders need substitution
- expedite budget can cover some but not all urgent orders
- warehouse capacity forces tradeoffs but does not make feasibility rare

This should let raw o4-mini produce some feasible plans while leaving
meaningful room for optimization.

Do NOT begin with catastrophic shortages where nearly every sensible plan
simply defers orders -- that would recreate the safe-action collapse seen
in Phase 4.

MINIMAL DATASET

For the first pilot:

| Split                | Scenarios |
|------------------------|-----------|
| Local development      | 20        |
| Model pilot             | 50        |
| Fine-tuning train       | 250-350   |
| Validation              | 50-75     |
| Final evaluation        | 120-150   |

Generate scenarios procedurally from fixed seeds. Split by scenario
configuration, not just random row, so evaluation includes unseen
inventory pressure and deadline combinations.

This is much smaller than the full disruption-recovery project while
still being substantial enough to measure behavior.

TWO ARMS

SFT arm:
- Ask gpt-5.5 for one plan per training scenario.
- Score it with the verifier.
- Allow at most three attempts.
- Keep the best feasible plan above a frozen threshold such as 0.75.
- Fine-tune gpt-4.1-mini on those plans.
- Record teacher attempts, score distribution, rejected scenarios, tokens,
  and cost.

RFT arm:
- Give o4-mini the same scenarios without target plans.
- Use the verifier as the RFT grader.
- Reward any feasible high-performing plan.
- Evaluate it on exactly the same final scenarios.

THE GO/NO-GO PILOT

Before building 300 training examples or submitting either fine-tuning
job, run only the 50-scenario pilot.

Proceed only when ALL of these hold:

Raw o4-mini:
- At least approximately 20% of plans are fully feasible.
- Imperfect plans receive a range of useful scores.
- It does not repeat one action pattern across nearly every scenario.

gpt-5.5 teacher:
- It does not produce near-optimal plans effortlessly on almost every
  first attempt.
- Its median plan quality leaves meaningful room for improvement.
- Obtaining strong labels requires retries or has a visible rejection
  rate.

Verifier:
- At least several materially different plans can score above 0.85.
- Constant strategies score materially below adaptive plans.
- Small improvements produce higher scores.
- Adversarial malformed plans cannot exploit the calculation.

Platform:
- Raw o4-mini must be deployable and directly evaluated.
- If it remains blocked, STOP. Do not repeat the Phase 4 experiment
  without its baseline.

WHAT WOULD INVALIDATE THE USE CASE

Reject this sliver before fine-tuning if:
- gpt-5.5 scores above approximately 0.90 on nearly every first attempt
- raw o4-mini almost never produces a feasible plan
- "defer everything" or another fixed policy receives a competitive score
- one obvious greedy rule solves almost every scenario
- all good plans look essentially identical
- a simple script can cheaply generate excellent SFT targets
- raw o4-mini cannot be deployed

This pilot should be inexpensive compared with another RFT job and
protects against committing to the wrong scenario.

WHAT A CONVINCING RESULT LOOKS LIKE

Report all five references:

| Arm                | Purpose                       |
|---------------------|--------------------------------|
| Raw gpt-4.1-mini     | SFT baseline                   |
| Raw o4-mini          | RFT baseline                   |
| gpt-5.5 teacher      | SFT label-quality ceiling      |
| SFT gpt-4.1-mini     | Imitation result               |
| RFT o4-mini          | Verifier-guided result         |

A convincing RFT outcome remains:
- RFT improves substantially over raw o4-mini
- RFT beats SFT by at least five absolute points
- the paired confidence interval excludes zero
- RFT beats the gpt-5.5 teacher's plan scores
- feasibility does not decline
- RFT uses multiple legitimate allocation patterns
- RFT wins particularly on unseen combinations of inventory pressure,
  deadlines, and expedite limits

EXPECTED BUILD EFFORT

The minimum implementation requires only:
1. a deterministic scenario generator
2. a JSON output schema
3. a Python plan verifier/scorer
4. a pilot runner for gpt-5.5, raw gpt-4.1-mini, and raw o4-mini
5. an SFT dataset builder
6. an RFT dataset/grader builder
7. a common evaluation script

No UI, database, solver, MCP server, or full supply-chain system is
needed.

THE FIRST MILESTONE should only be items 1-4 and the 50-scenario pilot.
That is enough to decide whether this use case deserves the cost of
